## 4. Water — the live engineering question
- City water NOT mandatory by code; a **private well is legal where public water isn't available/feasible** (NC Res. Code P2602.1; Plumbing 602.3). **Rainwater can't be the potable source** (P2901.1) — nonpotable only. [T-13]
- **But** a well on a closed landfill faces groundwater-contamination scrutiny + **Durham County Environmental Health** permitting; viable only if sited on the clean nub, far from waste, and it passes water-quality testing.
- A well removes the **water-main extension** cost but **NOT** the road/access requirement. [T-12,T-13]
- **Open:** can a private **driveway** off Clermont satisfy access instead of a built public road? (Planning question — big cost swing.)

## 5. Brownfields (optional liability tool)
- Ivanov (IVANOVSTROY LLC) is a **clean prospective developer** — didn't cause the contamination → eligibility gate passed. Need NOT own the land to apply. [T-10]
- **Free** to submit + get an eligibility ruling; **$2,000 only on a Letter of Eligibility**, $6,000 before execution. The agreement **caps liability** but also **imposes land-use restrictions**. [T-10]
- **Verdict:** probably skip for a 2-home build *unless* a future lender/buyer demands cover; revisit if the bigger vision survives (it won't — floodway). Full application DRAFT exists. [T-11]

## 6. Leanest viable path (if the nub checks out)
**Planning (or skip subdivision via a duplex) → Building & Safety + geotech → Stormwater/watershed → water (well or main) → driveway/road access**, plus one confirmatory Solid Waste call. A **duplex on one clean lot** avoids subdivision entirely.
Avoidable *only by staying off them*: Brownfields (decline), Floodplain (build outside flood line), Army Corps/DWR (don't touch wetland/buffer).
