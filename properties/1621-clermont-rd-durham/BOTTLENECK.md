## Primary bottleneck
**Profitability is undetermined because the all-in COST to make the small SW nub
buildable — a private ROAD/driveway on the unmaintained Clermont ROW + a CITY WATER-MAIN
EXTENSION + a mandatory full-depth ENGINEERED FOUNDATION on the disturbed substrate — is
unquantified.** These are the largest costs and the go/no-go decider, and they now sit
against a LOW exit ceiling (~$600–650K/home, T-30). We cannot price an offer until we have
a cost range.

**The water fork is RESOLVED — the wrong way.** Durham County Env Health (Eaton, T-33):
500-ft setback from the landfill limit → a private well is **effectively impossible** on
this parcel (nub is ~120–200 ft from waste; nothing is 500 ft away). So water = **city
water-main extension only (expensive)** — the cheap fork is closed. That removes a source
of upside and pushes the deal toward the kill line.

**What now decides it:** whether the all-in extraordinary cost can fit under the thin
residual at a near-free land basis. The extraordinary stack keeps GROWING: road/driveway
+ **city water-main extension** (well dead, T-33) + **full-depth engineered foundation**
(T-25) + **an NCDEQ-required REC environmental assessment and possible landfill-gas
mitigation even on the clean nub** (T-34) — all against a ~$600–650K exit ceiling and a
**permanent open-dump title notice** (T-34) that drags resale/financing. Remaining open
cost inputs: **Weist** (driveway vs. built road?), a **civil/geotech feasibility quote**
(road + main extension + foundation), and an **REC quote** (waste-boundary + soil/GW/gas).
Critical path: `Weist + Summit quote + main-extension cost + REC quote -> all-in cost -> go/no-go`.

**Read of the trend:** every agency answer since 7/2 has added cost or a constraint
(well dead, permanent notice, mandatory REC + gas). The deal is drifting toward the kill
line; the burden of proof is now on the land being nearly free.

**WATER IS NOW EXISTENTIAL (T-35):** well dead + rainwater can't be potable (NC Res. Code
P2901.1 — nonpotable only) → the **city water-main extension is the ONLY code-legal potable
source.** If it's uneconomic, the parcel is **unbuildable for a dwelling** — no potable water,
no home, no residential value. So the FIRST question to resolve is no longer "how much" but
"**is a main extension even feasible/affordable here at all?**" — get that from Durham Water
Management / PWEng before spending on anything else. The REC workup is ~$20–50k of pre-purchase
diligence (Phase I + Phase II + landfill-gas), itself a meaningful sunk cost.
